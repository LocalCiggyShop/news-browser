A NOTE ON PERSPECTIVE

What makes a story matter?

Our starting point is the seven news values: impact, timeliness, proximity, prominence, conflict, human interest, and the unusual. Simple rules look for evidence in publisher headlines and descriptions. Each story can explain the signals it matched.

Top stories combine 90% estimated newsworthiness with up to 10% coverage growth. The rules are imperfect: they cannot establish truth, severity or exact locations from a headline. Latest gives you publication-time ordering instead.

Coverage, not clicks.

Trending topics reflect growth across your observed feeds after four hours of history. They do not measure readership. Related reports may share a wire service or be grouped incorrectly.

Local processing.

Reading preferences, saved links and scoring stay on this device. Feed publishers receive requests from your browser and can see your IP address. We send no analytics and request no browsing history.

Free to read your way.

No backend, paid API or AI service is required. Original articles open on publisher sites and may have paywalls. Source availability and publisher terms can change.

A bounded memory.

Up to seven days and 1,600 feed items are cached, with a 6 MB item-data cap. Save up to 300 links separately. Clearing the feed cache keeps your reading list and preferences.
